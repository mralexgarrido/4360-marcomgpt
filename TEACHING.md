# Teaching with MarCom GPT

Use the lab to make AI-assisted work easier to explain and evaluate. The suggested activities below are facilitator options, not built-in course requirements or an automated grading system.

## A practical session

Begin with one station instead of asking learners to explore everything at once. Have them read the brief, identify the intended audience and consequence of an error, and draft a response in the prompt workbench. Compare the starter and a revised version, then discuss what improved and what the local score cannot evaluate.

Move to the station activities and illustrative outputs. Ask learners to identify claims that would need external verification. Finish with the quiz and a short explanation of how they would apply the framework to a real task.

## The seven-part prompt canvas

| Field | Question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome | What useful result should the work produce? |
| Audience | Who will use it, and what do they need? |
| Context | What background changes the answer? |
| Sources | What evidence or approved material should be used? |
| Constraints | What boundaries must the work respect? |
| Output | What format will make the result usable? |
| Verification | How will a person check the result? |

The implemented rubric is a structural teaching aid. A higher score does not establish factual accuracy, legal suitability, originality, or production readiness. Evaluate the learner's reasoning alongside the draft.

## Suggested discussion prompts

Ask learners what could go wrong even with a well-structured prompt, what evidence would change their decision, and what should remain a human responsibility. A useful peer-review activity is to have a partner rewrite only the verification field, then explain how that changes the workflow.

## Classroom data and access

Use fictional briefs and remove identifying information from examples. Browser-local progress is not a course gradebook; make submission and assessment requirements explicit outside the app. Allow an alternative written response where a particular interaction presents an access barrier, and report that barrier with reproducible details.

## Referencing the project
